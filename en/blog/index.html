The Ecosystem, with its own view for each section

EcosystemNew

DeFi, Games, Apps and bots and Other leave the usual table behind and get their own view, as Meme, AI and Tokenized assets already had. Each one looks at what matters for that kind of project.

DeFi

Projects are grouped (DEXs, lending, staking, derivatives…), with a menu at the top to jump between them. For each one: what it charges, what it keeps, what's deposited, what it returns to its token and how many times its token is worth what it earns in a year. At the top, a fact: of every $100 Solana DeFi charges, 63 go to Pump.fun and Jupiter. And a strip with what goes back to holders: buybacks and payouts in the last 30 days.

Games

Split into physical cards, gamified mining, games and a new group, Games of chance, for casinos and betting. Infinity Gaming and SOLPUMP move there, because by their own description they are casinos. The fact: the 3 most valuable game tokens are traded by about 150 people a day, and Collector Crypt, which charges almost $9M a month, by more than 2,000.

Apps and bots

Trading apps, Telegram bots, wallets and data, with each one's share of its group. Trading apps charge about 39 times more than bots, and almost none of them have a token: using them has nothing to do with buying one. Solflare moves here, to Wallets.

Other

DePIN, privacy, payments, tools and social. Render Network charges the most and returns almost all of it to its token; even so, its token is worth more than 100 times what it earns in a year.

And also

When a single wallet can sink a token

ScannerChartNew

This morning a widely followed Solana token fell more than 50% in five minutes: one wallet sold all at once. Looking at it with SolFlag we saw what we were missing: one wallet held almost half the token and it still showed yellow. We changed that.

One big wallet weighs more

On a meme, what the biggest wallet holds no longer subtracts in steps: it subtracts gradually, one point per point above 15%. And if one person's wallet (pools and exchanges excluded) holds 40% or more, the token is orange at best. That token goes from yellow with 60 points to orange with 39.

Whose it is and when it moved

The «One big wallet» flag now says whether it is one person's wallet and when it last moved («It moved today, at 17:19»), with its Solscan link.

Sudden crashes on the chart

A new pink mark: when the price falls more than 30% in one minute, it shows on the candle and in «What happened», with who sold at that moment, how many tokens and their approximate value. Facts from the chain, not accusations.

Also

  • At the top of the result, instead of «What subtracts: …», tags with what subtracts and its figure («One big wallet · 49.2%»).
  • The flags card only says its section: «Meme», «DeFi»…
  • The home page changes: instead of trending tokens (mostly one-day memes), the ecosystem right now: seven tiles (DeFi, games, apps, tokenized assets, AI, other and memes) that cycle through their projects and beat according to how their sector grew in 30 days.
  • On the home page, «Funds creators» is back in «Wallets of the day».
  • «How it works», with less repeated text.

ScannerEngineNew

Until now SolFlag judged a digital dollar as if it were a memecoin. Circle can freeze USDC or mint more: on a meme that's a red flag; on a regulated stablecoin, that's just how it works.

The chart, full screen: draw on it and see when the creator sold

ChartWho is behindNew

Today we reworked the chart of every token from top to bottom. It now loads better, opens full screen so you can draw on it, and above all it shows you something no ordinary chart tells you: in which candle the creator and the first buyers sold.

When the creator sold, right on the chart

Marks appear on top of the candles:

  • Creator (red arrow): when the wallet that created the token sells or dumps its tokens.
  • Launch buyers (orange): when those who bought in the first block sell. “×3” means three in the same candle.
  • Whales (blue): big buys and sells, worth more than 2% of the liquidity, or by the 10 biggest holders if you opened “Whales”.
  • You: when you scanned it.

Hover over a candle with marks to see who it was, how much and what share of their bag. Below the chart, “What happened” sums it up: how much the creator has sold, how many launch buyers are already gone, and the list in order, each with its Solscan link. Each kind of mark can be hidden.

It comes from the token’s latest trades and, if they don’t reach its birth, from the full history of the creator and of the biggest early buyers. We are careful with words: we say “sells” only if they got SOL back; if not, “dumps their tokens”, because they may have moved them to another wallet. And if it happened before graduating, we say so. As always, these are on-chain facts, not accusations.

Full screen, with drawing tools

The new chart icon opens it full screen (on a phone, the whole screen) with all its history loaded, zoom, log scale, candles or line, and volume. On the left you have TradingView-style drawing tools: trend and horizontal lines, rectangles, triangles, brush, text, ruler (how much it went up or down and in how long), Fibonacci and Gann box, with adjustable levels. Tap a drawing to change its colour, thickness or levels.

Your drawings are saved in your browser, for each token: open it again and they are still there.

A chart that loads better

  • Faster: we fetch the candles once and build the 1, 5 and 15-minute ones from them. Switching interval is now instant, and a brand-new token opens straight at 1 minute.
  • No more blank chart: for tokens that crashed, the chart never got drawn. Fixed. And if our data source is overloaded, it says so and tries again by itself.
  • The home page too: the trending and just-launched lists no longer stay empty when something fails; they retry within seconds.

The ones that went far were not luck; and five levels instead of three

DataWho is behindLevels

We had a question pending: of the tokens we see being born, which ones go far and who is behind them? With two weeks of records (13,344 tokens scanned at birth, from 29/10 to 12/11) it can now be answered. And the answer made us change how SolFlag scores.

Only 52 went far

At first we got about 200 tokens that passed $250,000 market cap in their first week. It wasn’t true: many “highs” were a single buy in an almost empty pool, which spikes the price for a minute. Counting only hours with real volume, they come down to 52, and 20 passed $1M.

  • What sets them apart most is the liquidity they had at birth. With less than $10,000, 3 of 12,384 made it; with $50,000 or more, 15%.
  • Going far is not lasting. 37 of the 52 hit their high in the first hour and 32 were dead at 24 h.
  • Behind serial launchers, nothing. Of the 1,449 launches paid by wallets with more than 20, none went far.

And many were moved by the same group

We followed the first buyers of those 52 and where they sent their SOL afterwards. In 10 of them, some buyers sent SOL to the wallet that created or paid for the token: 1,205 SOL in total. And 12 come in pairs: they were bought early by the same groups of wallets, which send SOL to each other. One example: two Gemini buyers send SOL to a wallet that, six minutes later, funds the creator of Rockstar.

This is what surprised us most: many of the ones that go far are not luck. That is why we look at who is behind and not only at the chart. The figures, the six pairs and their token pages are in Data and accuracy. These are on-chain facts, not accusations: sending SOL to the creator does not prove by itself who keeps the money.

Five levels instead of three

Looking at the records we saw something else: in new tokens, “high risk” and “medium risk” ended up the same (99.4% and 99.3% dead within a day). So the traffic light now says what each token is:

  • Diamond: a big project from our hand-reviewed list (Jupiter, Jito, Pyth…) or a trusted issuer (Circle, Paxos…).
  • Green: no alerts and 7 days old or more. A meme can be green; never diamond.
  • Yellow: one mild alert. It is also the most a token under 7 days old can get: with hours of life it hasn’t proven anything yet. We tell you from which day it could turn green.
  • Orange: danger without a trap, like very low liquidity.
  • Red: a trap: the creator can mint more or pull the liquidity, launches in series, the liquidity or volume is fake, or it copies another token.

Red and orange almost always fail at birth; the difference is the kind of danger. It’s on the website, in the Telegram bot and on the token pages, and explained in How it works.

Also these days

  • How it works: a page with what each flag checks, from which figure it turns on and why. Every traffic light in a scan has its “Why?”.
  • The Pump.fun curve: if a token is still on it, a box tells you what really happens there: fewer than 2 in 100 graduate, and 9 in 10 of those that graduate die within a day.
  • More useful records: the collector no longer scans tokens in order of arrival with seconds of life, but after half an hour and giving priority to those with liquidity. A quarter is still picked at random, so the percentages in Data and accuracy are not biased.

If you see anything odd, write to us at solflag.team@gmail.com or on X: @solflagTeam.

When an issuer changes its keys

IssuersSecurity

SolFlag trusts a short list of issuers: Circle, Paxos, Tether and xStocks. When you scan USDC it doesn’t treat it as a memecoin whose creator can freeze your tokens: it checks that the keys controlling the token are the ones Circle uses.

A few days ago someone asked us what happens if an issuer changes its keys. We answered quickly: “the chain rules”. But a new key on the chain proves the key changed, not who changed it. A legitimate change and a stolen key look the same from outside. And there was something worse: if Circle rotated a key, SolFlag stopped recognising USDC and flagged it as just another token.

What changes

  • If an official token from one of these issuers shows up with a key we don’t know, it comes out amber: “new key, pending review”. Neither green nor “fake”.
  • Every morning we read on the chain the keys of USDC, EURC, USDT, PYUSD and USDG. If one changes, we get an alert with the key, the transaction and who signed it.
  • A person checks it on the issuer’s own website before the token goes back to green. The reviewed list is public: emisores.json.

It’s slower than fully automating it. But “we don’t know yet” is an answer we prefer to give over an unchecked green.

And a mistake of ours with Ondo

While reviewing tokenized assets we saw that two official Ondo tokens, OUSG and USDon, came out as “probably spam”. They are products for approved investors only, with no public market, and the scanner mistook them for junk. Now Ondo is a verified issuer, with the addresses it publishes on its own website: its tokens come out as what they are, and their keys are also watched every morning.

If you see anything odd, write to us at solflag.team@gmail.com or on X: @solflagTeam.

Data and accuracy: all the numbers, the misses too

RecordsOpen data

Since 29 October, SolFlag scans every new token when it sees it and writes down how it ended at 24 hours and at 7 days. There are already more than 6,000. From today they are all on a new page, Data and accuracy, with the data to download and its format explained.

What they say, without spin

  • Almost everything new dies: 99.2% within a day. That’s why the reds dying proves little: reds and yellows die almost equally.
  • What separates the survivors is how big they were when we saw them: below $5,000, 0.1% survive; above $100,000, one in five. The traffic light doesn’t take this into account yet, and it will.
  • Who is behind matters too: of the 355 tokens paid for by wallets with more than 20 launches, none is still alive.
  • The two we marked green are still alive. They are too few to draw conclusions, but it’s the test we care about most, and it’s in plain sight on the page.

The tokens that are still alive are listed one by one: if we marked them red, those are our misses.

Correction of 6 November: a token off the Pump.fun curve whose liquidity is less than 0.2% of what it claims to be worth now counts as dead, because it can’t be sold. 2 tokens change in the days already published (DAKU, 1/11, and TSED, 3/11).

WebsiteEngine

A RugCheck false alarm, filtered out

EngineBot

The bot kept warning that a token went from medium to high risk because "only 0% of the liquidity is locked", and 15 minutes later that it was back to medium. When we checked, the problem wasn’t the token: RugCheck answered 0% in 5 out of 40 queries for a pool that was 99% locked.

Since version 0.3.2 of the engine, when RugCheck says less than 60% is locked, SolFlag asks up to two more times and keeps the highest value. A pool that really isn’t locked comes out low all three times, so that alert still works; only the false one is gone.

It’s a good example of why we don’t blindly trust any third-party data: what can’t be checked isn’t taken as good, and what looks odd gets checked again.
