How to check if a Solana token is safe before you buy

Thousands of tokens launch on Solana every day and almost all of them end badly. Before you buy, there are five things you can check on-chain. None of them tells you whether a token will go up; they tell you whether someone can take your money.

1

Can someone change the rules?

First, find out whether anyone can touch the token after you buy. It is written in the token's account (on Solscan, «Mint Authority» and «Freeze Authority»).

  • They can mint more (the mint authority is still active and belongs to a person): they can dilute your share to zero in a second.
  • They can freeze (the freeze authority is active): they can stop you from selling. Digital dollars carry it by law; in a memecoin it makes no sense.
  • It charges a fee to move or has Token-2022 extensions that change what you can do with your balance (transfer hook, frozen by default, non-transferable).

If the mint authority is active and belongs to a person, you don't need to look any further: that person decides what yours is worth.

2

Who holds it?

Look at how much the largest wallets hold. One person with a lot can crash the price with a single sale.

  • Worrying: a single wallet with more than 15% or the top 10 with more than 50%.
  • Very concentrated: a single wallet with more than 30% or the top 10 with more than 70%.

Before counting, trace every big account to its owner: a liquidity pool, a program or an exchange is not a person about to sell. And check whether several of the big ones got their SOL from the same wallet: it may be one person split up to look more decentralised than it is.

3

Will you be able to sell?

A price is not enough: there has to be real money on the other side so you can get out.

  • Low liquidity: under $50,000 is already delicate, and under $10,000 selling a normal amount moves the price a lot.
  • Inflated liquidity: when the real side of the pool (SOL, USDC or USDT) is less than 5% of what is shown. We saw a token showing $12.9M with $52 of real money behind it.
  • Unlocked liquidity in a market younger than 90 days: the creator can take the money out of the pool.
  • Inflated volume: when 80% or more of the volume comes from wallets that buy and sell the same amounts, the activity is fake.
4

Who is behind it?

One token doesn't show much; what its launcher did before does.

  • Serial launches: whoever paid for its creation launched 2 or more tokens in 24 hours. In 100 Pump.fun tokens, one wallet launched 7 copies of the same name in 10 minutes.
  • Track record: how many of their previous tokens never took off. If 80% of 3 or more went nowhere, this one probably won't either.
  • Copies: the same ticker as a famous token (USDC, SOL…) with a different address, or the same name as an older one with a bigger market. Always check the address, not the name.
5

What does it depend on?

Some tokens are worth what someone's promise is worth: Circle's dollar, the share an issuer holds in custody or the bridge that keeps the original. That's not bad, but you should know it: if the issuer or the bridge fails, so does the token. Check that the address is the one the issuer publishes on its official site, never one from an aggregator.

If it is still on the Pump.fun curve: in our registry (8,767 tokens) fewer than 2 in 100 graduate and, of those that do, 9 in 10 die within a day. Graduating is not doing well.

Frequently asked questions

Is a green token safe?

There are no guarantees. On SolFlag, green means the checks found no warning and the token has 7 days or more of market. A green token can fall and a red one can rise: the level tells you whether there are signs that someone could take your money.

Does locked liquidity make a token safe?

It helps, but it isn't enough. With locked liquidity the creator can't empty the pool, but they can still hold almost all of the supply, mint more if the mint authority is still active, or launch an identical token tomorrow.

How do I know it is the right address?

Copy the address from the project's official website or socials, never from a token search by name. There are copies with the same name and the same ticker; the only thing that can't be copied is the address.

Is this financial advice?

No. SolFlag is a guidance tool that explains what can be seen on-chain; it doesn't recommend buying or selling anything.
