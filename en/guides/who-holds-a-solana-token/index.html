How to see who holds a Solana token

An explorer's top holders list is misleading: many of those accounts aren't people. A liquidity pool, a program or an exchange show up at the top without being whales, and one person can hide split across several wallets. Here is how to tell them apart.

1

Look at the largest accounts, not the name

On Solscan, the token's «Holders» tab lists the accounts with the biggest balances. Careful: each line is a token account, not a wallet. You have to look at its «Owner» to know whose it is.

The top 20 are enough to see the split: if nobody there holds a lot, nobody further down does either.

2

Separate what isn't a person

  • Liquidity pools and programs (Raydium, Meteora, Orca, the Pump.fun curve…): they hold the market's tokens and won't sell on their own.
  • Named exchanges: it is their users' money. Shown, but not counted as whales.
  • Very active accounts (hundreds of moves in a few hours): usually bots, exchanges or market makers. They aren't hidden if they hold a lot: holding 10% or more is already a risk, whoever it is.
  • Named market makers (Wintermute, DWF Labs, with a published address): they do count, because they can sell a lot at once.
3

How much is too much

With what's left (the ones that look like people):

  • Worrying: a single wallet with more than 15% or the top 10 with more than 50%.
  • Very concentrated: a single wallet with more than 30% or the top 10 with more than 70%.

A real example from 15 Nov: in $STONK, 16 of the 20 largest accounts look like people and hold 24.5% of the supply; the largest has 5.4%. It is spread out.

In digital dollars and tokenized stocks the big wallets are the issuer's treasury, custodians and exchanges: PYUSD had 56% in a single one and that is normal.

4

Are several whales the same person?

One way to look more spread out than it is: a person creates several new wallets and sends them SOL from the same one. To spot it, check who paid for the first transaction of each big wallet. If several share that origin, they may be the same person or team.

It is a hint, not proof: if the origin is an exchange or a very busy account, lots of unrelated people get money from there, and it doesn't count.

5

Check who bought at launch

Some wallets buy in the first blocks of many launches. They are in 84% of launches, so having them says nothing about the token. What matters is whether they still hold tokens to sell: you see that if they show up among the largest holders.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the top holder usually a pool?

Because the liquidity pool holds the tokens that are bought and sold in that market. It isn't a person and won't sell on its own, so it doesn't count as a whale.

Is a wallet with a lot always a bad sign?

Not always: it can be an exchange, a custodian or an issuer's treasury. But in a memecoin, one person with more than 30% can crash the price with a single sale.

How do I know if several wallets belong to the same person?

Check who gave them their first SOL. If several of the largest received it from the same wallet, they probably share an owner or team. It is a hint worth checking on Solscan before drawing conclusions.
